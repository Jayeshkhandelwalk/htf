For your AI-based agricultural by-product analyser, you can extend the protein detection system to estimate residual gum, anti-nutritional factors (ANFs), and residual oil in guar meal, groundnut cake, almond cake, rice bran, and pulse residues.

The important thing is that these three properties need different reference tests and may need different sensors or chemical-analysis methods.

# 1. How to measure each parameter

A. Residual gum

Best approach: extraction and viscosity analysis, supported by a validated chemical assay.

- Extract water-soluble gum under controlled conditions.
- Measure the extract's viscosity using a viscometer or rheometer.
- For a quantitative gum concentration, use a validated extraction and gravimetric or carbohydrate-specific assay.
- Train the AI on NIR spectra labelled with the laboratory-measured gum content.

Output: estimated residual gum (% dry matter) and, separately, extract viscosity.

Note: Viscosity alone cannot determine gum concentration because molecular weight, temperature and other dissolved materials also affect it.

B. Anti-nutritional factors

There is no single test for all ANFs. Select the assay based on the material and intended food use.

- Phytate: validated colorimetric or enzymatic assay.
- Tannins: Folin-based or other validated tannin assays.
- Trypsin inhibitors: measure inhibition of trypsin activity using a suitable enzyme assay.
- Lectins: validated activity assay where relevant.
- Saponins: suitable validated chemical assay when relevant.

Output: separate predictions for each target, such as phytate in mg/g or trypsin-inhibitor activity in appropriate units.

The model should not combine all these into one generic “anti-nutrient percentage.”

C. Residual oil

Best approach: solvent extraction using a validated reference method, such as Soxhlet extraction or another appropriate total-fat method.

- Weigh the sample.
- Extract the fat using the prescribed laboratory procedure.
- Quantify the extracted material and calculate fat content on a dry-matter basis.
- Pair the measured values with NIR spectra to train the model.

Output: predicted residual oil (% dry matter).

Residual oil is particularly useful for comparing oilcakes because extraction efficiency affects their remaining fat content.

## 2. Hardware required

You can reuse much of the hardware from your protein estimation project.

| Parameter           | Main measurement hardware                                         | Optional supporting equipment                   |
| ------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Protein             | NIR spectrometer                                                  | Kjeldahl/Dumas reference analysis               |
| Residual gum        | Viscometer or rheometer; appropriate extraction equipment         | Analytical balance, temperature-controlled bath |
| Phytate and tannins | UV–Vis spectrophotometer or microplate reader, depending on assay | Centrifuge, pipettes                            |
| Trypsin inhibitor   | Spectrophotometer or microplate reader                            | Incubator and laboratory pipettes               |
| Residual oil        | Soxhlet apparatus or validated fat analyser                       | Analytical balance, drying oven                 |

These reference instruments are used to produce trustworthy labels for AI training. You do not necessarily need to purchase every instrument; your college laboratory or a testing laboratory may already have them.

Safety note: Solvent-based oil extraction and some chemical assays require trained laboratory supervision, appropriate ventilation and proper handling of chemicals.

## 3. Can one NIR spectrometer predict all four properties?

Potentially, yes. A suitable NIR instrument may provide useful signals related to protein, oil, moisture and certain carbohydrate properties. But prediction quality is target-specific.

| Target                     | NIR potential                                           | Important limitation                                                 |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Protein                    | Well-established application                            | Requires material-specific calibration                               |
| Residual oil               | Often promising                                         | Validate against measured fat content                                |
| Residual gum               | Possible, depending on composition and reference method | Water, fibre and other carbohydrates can interfere                   |
| Phytate                    | More challenging                                        | May not have sufficiently specific spectral information              |
| Tannins                    | Material-dependent                                      | Chemical composition varies considerably                             |
| Trypsin-inhibitor activity | Challenging                                             | Activity may not correlate reliably with a simple spectral signature |

For ANFs in particular, do not assume NIR can measure every compound accurately. If a target cannot be predicted with adequate independent validation, retain the laboratory assay rather than presenting an unreliable AI estimate.

## 5. Updated software stack

- Sensor acquisition: Python and the spectrometer's SDK.
- Data handling: pandas, NumPy and SciPy.
- Machine learning: scikit-learn, starting with PLS Regression and comparing SVR.
- Model validation: MAE, RMSE, \\(R^2\\), bias and target-specific uncertainty.
- Backend: FastAPI.
- Frontend: HTML, CSS, JavaScript and a charting library.
- Database: SQLite initially, with one record per sample and separate reference results for every target.

You can train a separate model for each target, or use a multi-output regression setup where justified. In either case, evaluate each output independently and validate on samples from unseen batches.

Each result should indicate whether it comes from a laboratory test or a validated AI prediction. A predicted composition profile is not a food-safety certification.

My recommendation: For the first working prototype, focus on protein + residual oil + moisture using NIR and laboratory reference data. Then add residual gum for guar meal and individual ANF assays for the relevant by-products. This staged approach gives you a much better chance of producing a demonstrable system with defensible accuracy.
